Betula: mark and wordmark

Betula is the birch, the pioneer tree of Lusatia's post-mining land, and the letters B, T and U stand in its name. The mark is a birch leaf, white, with the black marks of birch bark on it, on the green of the leaf: the four marks are the four bars of the first mark (birch bark that also read as the rows of a list), now entering the leaf from its edges, left and right in turn. It stands on its own in any shape a launcher cuts (circle, squircle, rounded square), in one colour for Android's themed icons, and down to 16 px. Running text says “Betula”; only the wordmark stresses B-T-U. The subtitle always says “inoffiziell”.

How the wordmark is spaced

Measured on Inter 800 (em): the stem of the T starts .252 from its origin, its bar ends .587 above the baseline, capitals are .728 high. Small capitals at .72 em reach .524, which leaves .063 of air under the bar; the E is pulled right until the same .063 lies between it and the stem. B–E, T–U and U–L are closed to about .07. Under 28 px the small cut takes over: small capitals at .68 em in weight 500, and .09 of air, so the E and the T do not clot at one pixel per gap.

How the mark is drawn

On a grid of 96 (design/logo/mark.mjs; icon.svg). The leaf is three cubic curves on its right, mirrored to the left: the tip at 48 / 14.7, widest at 67.6 (about two thirds down), the base at 68.2, a broad rounded wedge; the stem curves from 48 / 65.9 to 45.5 / 78.2, 3.6 wide. The whole glyph is 47 wide and 65 high and stands in the middle of the square. The four marks enter from the left and the right edge in turn, 4.7 high, 4.2 apart (tops at 31.3, 40.2, 49.1, 58), and end at 48.9, 50.4, 38.8 and 42.7: long, middle, short, longest, the rhythm of the first mark. They are cut to the leaf by computation, not by a clip path, so the app can draw the mark inline more than once on a page without ids.
